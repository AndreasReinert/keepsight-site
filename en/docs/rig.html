Filming with the big camera

The rig Keepsight is meant for: the camera records, the iPhone does the tracking.

A strong gimbal carries your mirrorless camera. The iPhone sits on top, looks in the same direction and acts as the eye: it detects the subject and drives the motors. The camera underneath takes the footage, with its sensor, its lenses and its resolution, for example 8K on a Lumix S1RII.

PartJob
CameraRecords the video or photos you will use later.
iPhone with KeepsightDetects the subject, keeps it centred and steers the gimbal.
GimbalCarries both and follows.

What you need

  • A camera gimbal that Keepsight controls. At present that is the FeiyuTech AK4500, rated by its maker for up to 4.6 kg. Gimbals with Apple DockKit are built for the iPhone alone and do not carry a camera.
  • A phone holder for the camera's hot shoe or a thread on the gimbal. It should tilt and lock firmly.
  • A camera and lens that stay below the payload limit together with the iPhone and holder.

Mounting and aligning

  1. Mount the camera with the lens you plan to use and put the iPhone into the holder, rear cameras facing forward.
  2. Balance the gimbal with everything that rides on it, including the iPhone. Switch it on only afterwards.
  3. Point the camera at a distant, distinct point such as the top of a church tower, so that it sits in the centre of the camera's image.
  4. Open Keepsight and adjust the holder until the same point lies on the cross in the middle of the viewfinder. Lock the holder.
  5. Connect the gimbal, see the chapter Supported gimbals.

The iPhone sits a few centimetres above the lens. For distant subjects such as aircraft this does not matter. For subjects a few metres away, the subject appears slightly higher in the camera than in the iPhone. In that case, align on a point at your typical working distance.

Choosing the iPhone lens

Keepsight keeps the subject in the centre of the iPhone's image. The narrower the iPhone's angle of view, the more precisely the centre is held, and the harder it is for the app to find a subject that is not in the picture yet.

Focal length on the camera (full frame)Lens on the iPhone
Up to about 35 mm1× (24 mm)
About 35 to 100 mm1× or the telephoto
Above 100 mmTelephoto (5× on iPhone 15 Pro Max, 120 mm)

Leave auto zoom off in this rig. It only changes the iPhone's picture, not the camera's.

Recording

  1. Choose the subject in Keepsight and tap Track.
  2. Start recording on the camera itself. Keepsight does not trigger the camera.
  3. Set focus and exposure on the camera, ideally with its own subject-detection autofocus.

To film with the iPhone as well, press its shutter too. The overlay video with frames and gimbal data is a good way to check afterwards how cleanly the tracking worked.

Speed with a heavy load

  • Start with Smooth or Standard. A heavy camera keeps swinging longer after hard commands.
  • For fast subjects such as low-flying aircraft choose Sport and check that the gimbal stays calm.
  • If the gimbal swings around the subject, lower the gain under Custom or widen the calm zone.
  • The longer the camera's focal length, the more every correction shows in the picture. Long lenses call for a softer setting.
