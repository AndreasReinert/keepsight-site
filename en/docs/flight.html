Flight data

Live flight data in the video from your own ADS-B receiver: what you need, where it works, privacy.

Availability: Live flight data is available only in approved countries and regions and needs your own ADS-B receiver. At present no region is approved, so the feature stays switched off everywhere.

With the flight data overlay, Keepsight shows the flight number, altitude and speed of the airliner you are following, in the viewfinder and in the video recorded by the iPhone. The data comes from your own ADS-B receiver on the same Wi-Fi. No internet and no flight data provider are involved.

Keepsight follows aircraft by sight without any receiver. The receiver is needed only for the overlay, and it is not part of the app: you buy or build it separately.

What you need

  • A receiver: a Stratux, for example a Raspberry Pi 3 with a 1090 MHz USB receiver stick and antenna, or another device that sends GDL90 over Wi-Fi.
  • Not a stick alone: a USB receiver stick such as the FlightAware Pro Stick needs a host, for example a Raspberry Pi running Stratux. A standard PiAware installation sends data to FlightAware and is not suitable.
  • The same Wi-Fi: the iPhone joins the receiver’s Wi-Fi. This network usually has no internet; that is fine.
  • An approved region: receiving the data yourself does not by itself mean the feature is permitted in a country.

Stratux or GDL90

ReceiverConnectionTested with Keepsight
Stratux (JSON)WebSocket to the receiver, usually at 192.168.10.1Not yet
GDL90 (UDP)The receiver sends to port 4000 on the iPhoneNot yet

Both are supported by protocol. No receiver has been tested with Keepsight on real hardware so far; individual devices, such as the SkyEcho 2, may behave differently. Traffic that is only sent by FLARM is not supported.

Switching it on

  1. Open the settings and switch on Flight data overlay (ADS-B).
  2. Read the notice and tap Agree and enable. Only then does iOS ask for your location.
  3. Join the receiver’s Wi-Fi, choose the receiver type and enter its address. iOS asks once for access to the local network.
  4. Tap Connect receiver. Keepsight never connects by itself.
  5. Choose Aircraft as the subject and point the camera at the aircraft.

If several aircraft fit the direction the camera is looking, Keepsight does not guess: tap Aircraft selection required and choose the right one.

You always choose the aircraft yourself, from the list of airliners currently received. The list shows the age of each position in seconds.

The settings show the state of the connection: connecting, connected without aircraft, receiving, or data out of date. Reconnect starts the connection again.

The flight data settings appear only while Aircraft is the selected subject.

Why Keepsight needs your location

iOS asks for your location only when you switch the flight data overlay on and agree. Keepsight needs it for two reasons:

  • Checking the region: whether flight data may be shown depends on the country you are in. Keepsight determines it from where the iPhone actually is, on the device itself.
  • Recognising the aircraft in the picture: Keepsight calculates the direction and the angle above the horizon from you to each aircraft and compares them with where the camera is pointing. The compass also needs the location to find true north.

Entering a place by hand is not offered, because the regional check would then no longer be reliable. If you do not use flight data, Keepsight never asks for your location.

Where it works

Keepsight determines the country on your iPhone from your current location. Your App Store country, language or internet address play no part. Near a border and at sea the feature stays off.

Every country is reviewed individually before it is approved. Until then the app tells you that live flight data is unavailable in your region. Tracking and recording work as usual.

What leaves your iPhone

Nothing. Keepsight only listens to the receiver and never changes its settings. Neither your location nor flight data is sent to a server.

Flight data exists only in the iPhone’s memory while the receiver is connected. It is deleted when you disconnect, choose another subject or leave the app. Nothing is stored or backed up.

Display

Flight data is shown only for scheduled passenger and cargo flights of known airlines. Private, business, military and government aircraft are never shown, and neither the registration nor the ICAO address of an aircraft appears.

The aircraft type is not part of ADS-B and is therefore not shown. Data that a receiver merely passes on, such as OGN, FLARM or TIS-B, is never shown either.

Choose the corner, compact or detailed, and metric or imperial units. Data older than 15 seconds is hidden instead of standing still. Every video with flight data carries the note “Flight data: Local ADS-B”.

The overlay is written into videos recorded with the iPhone. A camera on the gimbal records on its own and gets no overlay.

Optionally, Keepsight turns the gimbal towards the reported position of the selected aircraft while it is not yet visible in the picture. The compass limits the accuracy.

Accuracy

Flight data may be delayed, incomplete or wrong. It is for information only and not suitable for navigation, flight safety or operational decisions.
