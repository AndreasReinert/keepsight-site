Apple Watch

Remote control and status on your wrist.

What the watch does

The Apple Watch is a remote control and status display for the iPhone on the gimbal. It has no camera of its own, shows no gallery and stores nothing.

Open Keepsight on the iPhone, then on the watch. The watch reaches the iPhone over Bluetooth at about 10 m, further on the same Wi-Fi. Without a connection it says so instead of showing empty buttons.

Free

  • Shutter: photo, or start and stop video, with the countdown ticking on your wrist.
  • Switch between photo and video, start and stop tracking.
  • Status: mode, recording time in red, tracking state, gimbal and its battery.
  • Gestures page: all hand gestures at a glance. The watch taps your wrist whenever the iPhone recognises a gesture.
  • Double tap (watchOS 11 or later, Apple Watch Series 9, Ultra 2 or newer) triggers the shutter. On Apple Watch Ultra, set the Action button to Shortcut and choose Keepsight shutter.

With Keepsight Pro

  • Alerts on your wrist: target lost for 2 seconds, recording stopped, iPhone storage almost full, iPhone hot, gimbal battery low, gimbal disconnected.
  • Controls: zoom with the Digital Crown, pan and tilt with your finger, centre, next subject, whole group, pan program.
  • Highlights: a tap during recording marks the moment. In the gallery the marks appear as stars under the video; a tap jumps there.
  • Live view: a small picture with the detection frames, about 5 to 8 images per second. Tap a person to select the target. It is for checking, not filming, and switches off when the iPhone is hot.

One purchase unlocks everything: Keepsight Pro on the iPhone also unlocks the watch. The Pro preview unlocks the watch for its duration too. In the free version the only alert is when a recording ends at the 60-second limit.

Requirements

Apple Watch with watchOS 10 or later, paired with an iPhone with iOS 26 or later. Keepsight installs on the watch together with the iPhone app.

Not yet tested on a watch: The watch app follows Apple’s documentation. Double tap, the Action button and the live view still need to be tried on real watches.
